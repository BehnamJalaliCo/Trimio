# Premiere Pro / After Effects capabilities that define "professional" edit and motion-graphics quality (implementation-level notes for an automatic editor)

Research date: 2026-10-10. Current shipping versions: Premiere 26.5.x (Sept 2026; Adobe has renamed "Premiere Pro" to "Premiere" in its help center) and After Effects 26.x. Adobe's help pages (helpx.adobe.com) block direct fetches (Akamai 403), so I read them through a text-reader proxy. They are cited at their canonical helpx URLs, and each page's "Last updated" date is given where it matters. Facts from before 2024 are marked **[older]**.

## 1. Lumetri Color: pipeline order, auto color / match, working color space, gamma 2.4 vs 1.96, scopes, typical looks

### Takeaway
Lumetri is a fixed, top-down stack of operations: Basic Correction (Input LUT → White Balance → Tone → Saturation), then Creative (Look LUT + Faded Film, Sharpen, Vibrance, Saturation, Shadow/Highlight Tint, Tint Balance), then RGB Curves, then Hue-Saturation Curves (evaluated in parallel), then Color Wheels & Match, then HSL Secondary, then Vignette (last). Since 25.2 (April 2025) everything runs inside a color-managed pipeline: input color space → working space (default Rec.709 SDR via the "Direct Rec.709 (SDR)" preset, with automatic tone mapping and gamut compression of log, raw and HDR sources) → output space (Rec.709). Auto Color and Color Match are ML (Sensei) features that write their results into editable sliders and wheels; Color Match weights detected faces. The 1.96 vs 2.4 display-gamma mismatch on macOS is handled by a Viewer Gamma setting (2.4 broadcast, 2.2, or 1.96 macOS) and by Display Color Management.

### Cited Findings

**Processing order (official)**
- "The Lumetri Color panel processes from top down. Basic, Creative, and RGB Curves are processed before feeding into the Hue Saturations Curves." Sections after curves (Color Wheels, HSL Secondaries, Vignette) are not considered when the curve eyedropper samples. "The Hue Saturation Curves process in parallel. All the curves sample the color value at the time it feeds into the Hue Saturation Curves section." Example: if Hue vs Hue turns green into blue, a Hue vs Luma sample still lands on the *green* section of the curve. — [Adobe: Hue and Saturation curves](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/correct-color/add-color-effects/correct-color-using-hue-and-saturation-curves.html)
- Vignette: "The vignette applies after all other color corrections, making it ideal as a finishing touch." Its controls are Amount (negative darkens the edges), Midpoint (lower values affect more of the frame), Roundness (negative = more rectangular, positive = more circular), and Feather (edge softness). Adobe advises subtle values. — [Adobe: Vignettes using Lumetri](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/add-video-effects/commonly-used-effects/create-vignettes-using-lumetri-color.html)
- Adobe's recommended workflow order: Basic Correction (LUT + technical exposure fixes) → Creative (Looks) → Curves (RGB + Hue/Sat) → Color Wheels & Match → Color Match between shots → vignette last. — [Adobe: Color correction workflow](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/correct-color/color-correction-fundamentals/color-correction-workflow.html)

**Basic Correction controls (official)**
- Input LUT slot (a starting LUT, preset or custom). White Balance: eyedropper on a neutral area, Temperature (left = cooler), Tint (negative = green, positive = magenta). Tone: Exposure, Contrast (mainly affects midtones), Highlights / Shadows (adjust while "minimizing clipping"), Whites / Blacks (set the clipping points), Saturation (applied to all colors equally). "Auto … sets the sliders to maximize the tonal scale and minimize highlight and shadow clipping." — [Adobe: Basic color correction options](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/correct-color/color-correction-fundamentals/basic-color-correction-options.html)
- Auto Color "uses Adobe Sensei ML technology" to set exposure, white balance and contrast. The results appear in the regular Basic Correction sliders, and an **Intensity** slider scales the whole auto adjustment. It works on LOG footage and on footage with LUTs applied. — [Adobe: Use Auto Color](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/correct-color/add-color-effects/use-auto-color.html) (updated Aug 18, 2026)
- Community bug reports say the Lumetri Auto button fails when Lumetri sits *below* Warp Stabilizer in the effect stack. A Lumetri color-space transform placed before Warp Stabilizer also makes stabilization analysis very slow. The workaround is to stabilize first (or nest) and then grade. — [Adobe community bug](https://community.adobe.com/t5/premiere-pro-bugs/auto-button-in-lumetri-doesn-t-work-properly-after-warp-stabilizer-applied-to-clip/idi-p/15464983); [Adobe community bug 2](https://community.adobe.com/t5/premiere-pro-bugs/p-warp-stabilizer-very-slow-when-using-color-space-conversion/idi-p/14639782)

**Creative section (official)**
- Look (LUT) with **Intensity**. **Faded Film** (vintage lift). **Sharpen** (0 = off; Adobe warns against unnatural over-sharpening). **Vibrance** ("changes the saturation of all lower-saturated colors with less effect on the higher-saturated colors… also prevents skin tones from becoming oversaturated"). **Saturation**. **Shadow Tint / Highlight Tint** wheels (split-toning). **Tint Balance**. — [Adobe: Apply Lumetri looks](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/correct-color/add-color-effects/apply-lumetri-looks.html)
- Since 25.2 the Look menu is color-space aware. The bundled Look LUTs were designed for Rec.709, so in a wide-gamut working space you must tag them "Rec.709 (scene)". The LUT preview thumbnails are not color-managed. — [Adobe: Color Management and Lumetri Color](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/correct-color/set-up-color-management/lumetri-enhancements.html)

**Curves, Wheels, HSL (official)**
- RGB Curves: a master (luma) curve plus R, G and B channels. An S-curve is Adobe's standard "natural-looking contrast" technique. The Hue Saturation curves are Hue vs Sat, Hue vs Hue, Hue vs Luma, Luma vs Sat and Sat vs Sat; the eyedropper places 3 control points automatically. — [Adobe: RGB curves](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/correct-color/add-color-effects/correct-color-using-rgb-curves.html); [Adobe: Hue/Sat curves](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/correct-color/add-color-effects/correct-color-using-hue-and-saturation-curves.html)
- Color Wheels: three-way (shadows, midtones, highlights). Each wheel adjusts hue/saturation, and a slider beside it adjusts brightness. — [Adobe: Color wheels](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/correct-color/add-color-effects/correct-color-using-color-wheel.html)
- HSL Secondary: a key built from H, S and L ranges (top triangles set the range, bottom triangles the softness) with set, add and remove eyedroppers. Refine has **Denoise** and **Blur** for the matte. The Correction controls are a midtone wheel (or a 3-way wheel), Temperature, Tint, Contrast, Sharpen and Saturation. Typical uses: skin tone fixes and individual objects. — [Adobe: HSL Secondary](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/correct-color/add-color-effects/correct-color-using-hsl-secondary-controls.html)

**Shot matching (official)**
- Color Match: Comparison View (side-by-side or split) with a reference frame and the current frame. **Face Detection is on by default**: "if Auto Color detects faces in either the reference or current frame it gives higher weight to the colors in the facial region," which gives better skin matches against distracting backgrounds. The match is written into **Color Wheels and the Saturation slider**. It matches the *current frame only*, so pick a representative frame. — [Adobe: Match color between shots](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/correct-color/add-color-effects/match-color-between-shots.html)

**Color management / working space (25.2+, official)**
- The pipeline is input color space → working color space (all effects and Lumetri run here) → output color space (Program Monitor, scopes, transmit and export all show the output-space image). — [Adobe: How Color Management works](https://helpx.adobe.com/in/premiere/desktop/correct-color/set-up-color-management/how-color-management-works.html); [Adobe community announcement](https://community.adobe.com/announcements-732/now-released-dramatically-updated-color-management-in-premiere-pro-313927)
- The default preset is **Direct 709 (SDR)**: Rec.709 SDR working space, Rec.709 output, SDR media passes through, and wide-gamut raw/log sources are tone-mapped and gamut-compressed *on input*. Older pages call the same preset "Broadcast 709 (SDR)". The alternatives are **Wide Gamut (Tone Mapped)** (ACEScct working space, tone mapping on output), Direct HDR (HLG), Direct PQ (HDR), and Disable Color Management (manual LUT workflow). Adobe says never to change the working space after grading. — [Adobe: Configure sequence color management](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/correct-color/set-up-color-management/configuring-sequence-color-management.html)
- Tone-mapping algorithms: **Hue Preservation (default)**, with an adjustable **Knee** and **Highlight Saturation (default 0.5)**; **By Channel** (smooth, slightly darkens, desaturates highlights); **Max RGB** (smooth, keeps more highlight saturation). Gamut compression: **Luminance Preserving (default)** or Saturation Preserving. Input tone mapping happens *before* effects (highlights can become unrecoverable); output tone mapping happens *after* all grading. Default graphics white is 100 nits for SDR. — [Adobe: Color Management options](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/correct-color/set-up-color-management/color-management-options.html)
- Auto tone mapping is on by default, so iPhone HLG, PQ and log footage (S-Log, C-Log, V-Log) no longer look blown out in Rec.709 sequences. — [Adobe: Tone mapping](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/correct-color/set-up-color-management/tone-mapping-in-premiere.html)
- Supported log inputs include Apple Log, ARRI LogC3/C4, Canon Log 1/2/3, DJI D-Log, Fuji F-Log, Leica L-Log, Panasonic V-Log, RED Log3G10, Sony S-Log/2/3, and ACEScct. MP4/MOV/MXF often lack usable color metadata, so clips may need manual tagging. — [Adobe: Color Management options](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/correct-color/set-up-color-management/color-management-options.html)
- "Color space aware effects" (Lumetri, Cross Dissolve, Film Dissolve, Dip to Black, Track Matte Key, Tint, Black & White and others) behave differently per working space. — [Adobe: Color Management options](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/correct-color/set-up-color-management/color-management-options.html)
- A colorist in the community thread says the Lumetri curves clamp in ACEScct and Dip to Black does not reach ACEScct black (negative values). Adobe acknowledged problems with tone mapping of SDR media in wide gamut. — [Adobe community announcement thread](https://community.adobe.com/announcements-732/now-released-dramatically-updated-color-management-in-premiere-pro-313927)

**Gamma 2.4 vs 1.96 (QuickTime/macOS "washed-out" problem)**
- Premiere (beta v24, 2023) added **Viewer Gamma**: broadcast 2.4 or macOS 1.96. It lives in the Lumetri Settings tab, Project / Team Project / Production settings, and Media Encoder color preferences. For native Rec.709 it applies everywhere video is displayed; for color-managed (log) media it affects only the Program Monitor. — [Adobe community beta post](https://community.adobe.com/t5/premiere-pro-beta-discussions/released-now-in-beta-selectable-viewer-gamma/m-p/14002424/highlight/true); [Adobe Premiere Elements: Color correction settings (1.96/2.2/2.4; 1.96 is macOS-only)](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere-elements/desktop/color-correction-and-grading/color-correction-settings.html)
- Current Premiere Color Settings expose Viewer Gamma, HDR Graphics White (nits), 3D LUT Interpolation, Auto-detect log/raw, and Color Space Aware Effects. Preferences include Display Color Management and Extended Dynamic Range Monitoring. — [Adobe: Color settings](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/correct-color/color-correction-fundamentals/color-settings.html); [Adobe: Color preferences](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/get-started/preferences-and-settings/color-preferences.html)
- **[older, 2021]** Oliver Peters: with Display Color Management OFF, viewer brightness and contrast match Rec.709 on an external monitor and in QuickTime, but the viewer looks oversaturated (especially reds), so exports look "washed out." ON gives a better saturation match but crushes the shadows. Web-delivery workarounds: (a) a separate web export with a Lumetri adjustment layer (more saturation, slightly lower shadows), or (b) in AE, a Rec.709 2.4 working space with the render-queue output profile set to sRGB. — [ProVideo Coalition, Mar 2021](https://www.provideocoalition.com/understanding-premiere-pros-color-management/)

**Scopes (official)**
- Lumetri Scopes: Vectorscope YUV (hue as angle, saturation as radius, with target boxes); Histogram (default output-range lines at CIN code values 95–685); RGB Parade (RGB, YUV, RGB-White, YUV-White); Waveform in RGB, Luma (−20 to 120 IRE), YC and YC-no-chroma modes. — [Adobe: Available Lumetri Scopes](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/correct-color/add-color-effects/available-lumetri-scopes.html)
- Skin tones of every ethnicity fall near the vectorscope "skin tone line" between red and yellow. Boris FX documentation puts it at 123° (NTSC standard) and notes skin sits slightly clockwise of it. — [Larry Jordan: Reading video scopes](https://larryjordan.com/articles/learn-how-to-read-video-scopes/); [Boris FX scope docs](https://cdn.borisfx.com/borisfx/Documentation/vegas/2026/en/content/topics/8-design/video_scope_settings.htm). A forum reply suggests skin at roughly 40–70 IRE on the luma waveform (low authority). — [Creative COW](https://creativecow.net/?p=1982922)

**New Color mode (Premiere beta, 2026)**
- "Color mode" (beta in the 26.2 timeframe) adds a dedicated Color Monitor, a Clip Grid and a Color Controls panel. Its controls are 2-D drag controls, each moving two parameters (vertical and horizontal). Style modules include: Color & Contrast (Exposure keeps the black point pinned; Temperature+Tint as one 2-D control; Balance; Saturation); Color Shift (Sat, Hue and Lum shift per hue with adjustable width); Contrast Kit (Compression = S-curve with pinned black/white; Range = linear contrast about a pivot; Highlights); Detail (Texture in 5 frequency bands, Sharpness); Film Color (negative + print stock pair, Strength, Midtone Protection for skin, highlight and shadow protection and balance); Flare (raises the black point, Slope). Not recommended for client work yet. — [Adobe: Color mode (beta)](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/correct-color/color-mode-fundamentals/color-mode-basics.html); [Adobe: Available Style modules](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/correct-color/color-mode-fundamentals/available-style-modules.html); [Adobe release notes (May 2026, 26.2.x)](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/whats-new/release-notes.html)

### Inferences
- An automatic editor that wants to match Lumetri should implement the same fixed node order, evaluated in float: input transform/LUT → WB (Temp/Tint) → exposure/contrast/HL/SH/W/B → saturation → look LUT × intensity → vibrance/split-tone → RGB curves → hue curves (parallel sampling of one input) → 3-way lift/gamma/gain → HSL-keyed secondary → vignette → output transform.
- "Auto tone" can be approximated by stretching the histogram to minimize clipping (Adobe's own description). "Match" can be approximated by per-region (lift/gamma/gain) mean and variance matching with a face-region weight.
- For talking-head social video, the evidence-backed "pro" recipe is: correct white balance on a neutral, skin near the skin-tone line, vibrance rather than saturation (it protects skin), a gentle S-curve, a subtle negative vignette, and a Rec.709 output tagged for gamma 2.4 or a web-compensated variant. Anything stronger (teal-orange, faded film) is a creative layer applied after correction.
- To avoid the "washed-out on Mac/QuickTime" complaint, tag outputs correctly (BT.709 primaries, transfer and matrix in the bitstream) and consider whether the target viewer applies 1.96 gamma.

### Gaps
- Lumetri's exact numeric slider ranges and defaults (for example Exposure ±5 stops, Temperature scale) and the internal math of Highlights/Shadows (local vs global) are not documented in the pages I could read.
- No primary Adobe source gives "typical pro grading values" for talking-head social video. The skin IRE ranges above are community advice only.
- The Auto Color / Color Match ML algorithm is not described beyond "Sensei ML" and "face weighting."

## 2. Essential Graphics / MOGRT, AE text animators, and speech-to-text captions (2024–2026)

### Takeaway
AE text animation is defined by *animator groups* (the properties to animate, set to their *end* values) driven by *selectors* (Range, Wiggly, Expression) that compute a per-character (or word or line) weight 0–100%. The selector shape (Square, Ramp Up/Down, Triangle, Round, Smooth) and Ease High/Low define the "cascade" feel. Templating happens through the Essential Graphics panel (Essential Properties, Responsive Design: Time with protected intro/outro regions) exported as .mogrt. Premiere's caption pipeline is Speech-to-Text transcript → Create Captions (preset, max chars, min duration, gap, 1 or 2 lines, and since June 2026 a **Single Word** layout) → track styles in the Properties panel (fill, multiple strokes, multiple shadows, text-as-mask, responsive position) → cloud translation (Google/Microsoft engines).

### Cited Findings

**AE text animators and selectors (official)**
- Workflow: add an animator (property), use a selector to decide how much each character is affected, and set the animator property to its *end* value. Usually you keyframe only the selector. Example: Opacity animator = 0, then keyframe Range Selector End from 0% → 100%. — [Adobe: Animating text](https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/desktop/animating-text/text-animation/animating-text.html)
- Common selector properties: **Mode** (Add default, Subtract, Intersect and others, combined like masks), **Amount** (0–100% scales the animator's effect), **Units** (Percentage or Index) and **Based On** (Characters, Characters Excluding Spaces, Words, Lines). With Characters, spaces count, which "effectively pauses the animation between words." — [Adobe: Animating text](https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/desktop/animating-text/text-animation/animating-text.html)
- Range selector: **Start / End / Offset**; **Shape** = Square, Ramp Up, Ramp Down, Triangle, Round, Smooth; **Smoothness** (character-to-character transition time with Square); **Ease High / Ease Low** (100% = eases gradually while fully→partially selected (High) or partially→unselected (Low); −100% = abrupt); **Randomize Order** + **Random Seed** (0 = seed derived from the animator group). — [Adobe: Animating text](https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/desktop/animating-text/text-animation/animating-text.html)
- Wiggly selector: Max/Min Amount, Wiggles/Second, Correlation (100% = all characters move together, 0% = independent), Temporal/Spatial Phase, Lock Dimensions. Expression selector: the default Amount expression is `selectorValue * textIndex/textTotal`, evaluated once per character, with `textIndex`, `textTotal` and `selectorValue` available. — [Adobe: Animating text](https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/desktop/animating-text/text-animation/animating-text.html)
- Animator properties include Anchor Point, Position, Scale, Rotation (per-axis with Per-Character 3D), Opacity, Fill/Stroke color, Tracking, Line Anchor (0% left / 50% center / 100% right), Character Offset (Unicode shift; "Preserve Case & Digits"), and Blur (separate H/V Gaussian). **Anchor Point Grouping** (Character/Word/Line/All) plus Grouping Alignment control the pivot. Adobe recommends enabling layer motion blur for animated text. Bundled text presets were authored at 720×480 with 72-pt Myriad Pro, so position values need rescaling. — [Adobe: Animating text](https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/desktop/animating-text/text-animation/animating-text.html)
- AE 26.0 (Jan 2026) added keyframable **variable-font axes** (weight, width, slant and others) for motion typography, plus SVG import as native shape layers. AE 26.3 (June 2026) added a variable-font-only filter. — [CG Channel, Jan 21 2026](https://www.cgchannel.com/2026/01/adobe-releases-after-effects-26-0-with-native-substance-support/); [Adobe: Working with variable font axes](https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/desktop/variable-font-axes/work-with-variable-font-axes/working-with-variable-font-axes.html)

**Essential Graphics / MOGRT templating (official)**
- **Responsive Design – Time**: protected regions (from the timeline, the work area, or comp markers with "Protected Region" on) keep intro/outro durations fixed while the unprotected middle stretches when the comp is nested or exported as a MOGRT. In Premiere you change the MOGRT length directly without time remapping. A MOGRT containing audio does *not* stretch adaptively. — [Adobe: Responsive design – time](https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/using/responsive-design.html)
- **Essential Properties**: properties exposed in the Essential Graphics panel become overridable per instance on nested comps or MOGRT instances. — [Adobe: Essential Properties](https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/using/essential-properties.html); [Adobe: Create MOGRTs](https://helpx.adobe.com/my_en/after-effects/desktop/motion-graphics/work-with-motion-graphics-templates/creating-motion-graphics-templates.html)
- In Premiere, **Responsive Design – Position** pins a graphic to the frame edges or to another layer, so graphics adapt when the aspect ratio changes (useful after Auto Reframe). Premiere also has "preserve intro and outro animations" for responsive graphics. — [Adobe: Create styles for captions](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/add-text-images/insert-captions/create-styles-for-captions.html); [Adobe: Create responsive graphics](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/add-text-images/insert-images-and-graphics/create-responsive-graphics.html)

**Speech-to-Text and caption generation (official)**
- Transcribe options: language (downloadable language packs), speaker labeling, audio analysis (clips tagged Dialogue, or a specific track), In-to-Out only, and merge with the existing transcript. Media can be auto-transcribed on import. — [Adobe: Speech to Text](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/add-text-images/insert-captions/auto-transcribe-video-using-speech-to-text.html) (updated Jun 2, 2026)
- 26.2 (May 2026): "Transcriptions are now faster and more accurate, with improved speaker attribution and better handling of overlapping dialogue." — [Adobe release notes](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/whats-new/release-notes.html)
- Create Captions dialog options: caption preset ("Subtitle default"), format (subtitle or broadcast 608/708/Teletext with streams), style, **max length in characters, duration, gap between captions**, and lines (single or double). — [Adobe: Create captions](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/add-text-images/insert-captions/create-captions.html)
- Community-tested ranges (not official): max length 7–72 characters (the cap rose from 42 to 72 in PR 22.0), min duration 1.2–6 s, gap 0–10 frames. Testers report the settings interact unpredictably (min duration overrides max length; wrapping depends on text-box and font size). — [Adobe community thread](https://community.adobe.com/t5/premiere-pro-discussions/auto-captions-character-length-setting-does-not-work-right/td-p/14000960); [Adobe community bug](https://community.adobe.com/t5/premiere-pro-bugs/create-captions-minimum-duration-in-seconds-being-completely-ignored/idc-p/14082963)
- **Single-word captions** (26.3, June 2026): Create Captions → Captioning Preferences → Layout → Single Word gives one word per caption, synced to speech, "for fast-paced, social-first content," exported "exactly as they appear in your preview." — [Adobe: Single word captions](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/add-text-images/insert-captions/create-single-word-captions.html); [Adobe release notes June 2026](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/whats-new/release-notes.html)
- Caption styling (Properties panel): Fill; **multiple Outer/Inner/Center strokes**; **multiple shadows**; Mask with Text; align and transform; **Track styles** for captions and **Linked styles** for titles (saved to the project or locally); Responsive Design – Position; "Redefine style → All captions on track." — [Adobe: Create styles for captions](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/add-text-images/insert-captions/create-styles-for-captions.html); [Adobe: Translate captions](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/add-text-images/insert-captions/translate-captions.html)
- Caption translation uses a cloud service with "third-party translation models (Google Translate and Microsoft Translator)" and creates a new caption track per language (Alt/Option-click isolates a track). It launched in 25.2 (April 2025) with **27 languages**. — [Adobe: Translate captions](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/add-text-images/insert-captions/translate-captions.html); [Adobe press release via BusinessWire, Apr 2 2025](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250402472642/en)
- Text-Based Editing: filter the transcript for Filler words, Pauses or Speakers, then Delete / Delete all (bulk jump-cut removal). 26.5 (Sept 2026) added **Paper Edit** (build a sequence by selecting transcript lines). — [Adobe: Detect and delete pauses](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/edit-projects/edit-video-using-text-based-editing/detect-and-delete-pauses-in-transcripts.html); [Adobe release notes Sept 2026](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/whats-new/release-notes.html)
- 25.x added preferences for default fonts for text and captions and "new animation tools" (animate layers from the Properties panel). — [Adobe release notes](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/whats-new/release-notes.html)

### Inferences
- The AE selector model maps directly onto a per-glyph weight function: w_i = Amount × Shape((i − start)/(end − start) + offset) with easing, combined across selectors by Add, Subtract or Intersect. Implementing it gives the "pro" staggered text reveals (typewriter, cascade fade-up, word pops) that most motion designers build in AE.
- A caption engine at Premiere parity needs: word-level timestamps; chunking by max chars (~32–42 for vertical social is common practice, but Adobe does not mandate a value), min duration and gap; 1 or 2 lines; single-word mode; track-level styles with multiple strokes and shadows; safe-area placement responsive to aspect ratio.
- Protected-region time remapping (fixed intro/outro, stretchable hold) is the key behavior for auto-fitting lower-thirds and titles to arbitrary durations.

### Gaps
- Default values of the "Subtitle default" caption preset (exact max chars, min duration, gap) are not stated in current Adobe docs.
- The ASR engine behind Speech-to-Text is not publicly specified, and no WER figures are published.
- I could not fetch the AE "Create MOGRTs" page body, so details of exposed property types (dropdown menus, media replacement, Source Text) are only summarized from search snippets.

## 3. Auto Reframe, Morph Cut, Warp Stabilizer, Scene Edit Detection, Remix, Enhance Speech, auto-ducking, loudness

### Takeaway
Each "assistive AI" tool writes standard, editable parameters rather than baking pixels:
- Auto Reframe writes Motion keyframes into a duplicated sequence, with three tracking-speed presets.
- Morph Cut uses face tracking plus optical-flow interpolation.
- Warp Stabilizer falls back from Subspace Warp → Perspective → Position/Scale/Rotation → Position.
- Ducking writes gain keyframes into an Amplify effect.
- Auto-Match sets clip gain to −23 LUFS.
- Remix rearranges music using beat-similarity analysis and crossfades, landing within about 1 s of the target.

### Cited Findings

**Auto Reframe**
- Reframes clips or whole sequences to square, vertical or 16:9, or crops 4K+. Sequence → Auto Reframe Sequence makes a duplicate sequence (stored in an "Auto Reframe Sequences" bin) with the effect on every clip. Since 26.2 (May 2026) you can set a custom **target resolution**. — [Adobe: Auto Reframe overview](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/add-video-effects/commonly-used-effects/auto-reframe-overview.html) (updated Apr 15, 2026)
- Motion Tracking presets: **Slower Motion** (talking heads; "almost static… very few keyframes"); **Default** (follows action, weak on very fast motion); **Faster Motion** (sports; "adds a lot of keyframes"). The optional **Clip Nesting** preserves existing motion keyframes and speed changes, but nesting breaks soft transitions because handles are lost. Complex shots may need manual keyframe fixes. — [Adobe: Auto Reframe for sequences](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/add-video-effects/commonly-used-effects/add-auto-reframe-effect-to-a-sequence.html)

**Morph Cut**
- Uses "a combination of face tracking and optical flow interpolation." Results get blotchy with background, hand or body movement, tight framing, or profile faces. — [Adobe: Morph Cut overview](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere-pro/using/morph-cut.html) **[older page text]**
- Ideal input: a single talking head on a static background. Analysis starts automatically in the background ("Analyzing in background" banner) and re-runs on any trim or undo. The transition is symmetric about the cut and defaults to the 30-frame Video Transition Default Duration, but "a typical transition duration that works well is about **10–20 frames**." Cut where words peak or pause to avoid lip-sync problems. — [Adobe: Apply Morph Cut](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/add-video-effects/apply-video-transitions/apply-morph-cut-to-smoothen-jump-cuts.html) (updated Jan 7, 2026)

**Warp Stabilizer (official parameter list)**
- Result: **Smooth Motion (default)** or No Motion. Smoothness: higher = smoother, and >100 needs more crop. Method: Position → Position/Scale/Rotation → Perspective (corner pin) → **Subspace Warp (default)**; each falls back to the simpler method if there are too few track points. Preserve Scale. Framing: Stabilize Only / Stabilize+Crop / **Stabilize, Crop, Auto-scale (default)** with Maximum Scale and Action-Safe Margin / Stabilize, Synthesize Edges (fills edges from neighboring frames; Synthesis Input Range in seconds, Edge Feather, Edge Cropping default 0 px). Additional Scale avoids an extra resample. Advanced: Detailed Analysis; **Fast Analysis (on by default)**; Rolling Shutter Ripple (**Automatic Reduction default** or Enhanced); Crop Less ↔ Smooth More. Analysis ignores other effects on the same clip. Subspace Warp can warp unwantedly and Perspective can keystone; in that case use a simpler method. — [Adobe: Warp Stabilizer settings](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/add-video-effects/commonly-used-effects/warp-stabilizer-settings.html)

**Scene Edit Detection**
- Detects original cut points inside a single file. Options: add a cut at each point, create a bin of subclips, or add clip markers. Linked audio is cut too unless it is separated first. — [Adobe: Scene Edit Detection](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/edit-projects/change-clip-sequence/detect-edit-points-using-scene-edit-detection.html) (updated Aug 18, 2026)

**Remix (music retiming)**
- "Premiere measures multiple qualities of each beat in the song and compares them against every other beat" to find transition points and loops. It then cuts "at musically appropriate moments and adds crossfades." The beginning and end are **always preserved**; only the middle is edited. Edits are shown as zig-zag lines on the clip. — [Adobe: Remix in Premiere](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/add-audio-effects/apply-audio-effects/remix-in-premiere.html) (updated Aug 18, 2026)
- Parameters: Target Duration; **Segments** (fewer = minimal cuts; more = flexibility for dynamic songs); **Variations** (melodic vs harmonic emphasis). Accuracy: "within 5 seconds of the target duration, typically within 1 second." It does not understand lyrics and may splice verses. To hit a sync point, split at the moment and remix both halves. — [Adobe: Remix tool considerations](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/add-audio-effects/apply-audio-effects/remix-tool-considerations-in-premiere.html)

**Enhance Speech**
- AI dialogue cleanup (noise reduction plus vocal "presence") in Essential Sound → Enhance. It runs in the background with a progress bar, and a **Mix Amount** slider blends enhanced and original audio. — [Adobe: Apply Enhance Speech](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/add-audio-effects/adjust-volume-and-levels/enhance-speech.html) (updated Jan 7, 2026)
- It is the same feature as Adobe Podcast's web Enhance Speech, "optimized to perform locally… no internet connection required." It does not work well with loud background music, overlapping speakers, whispers or singing. Users report over-compression and "underwater" artifacts above about 0.3 mix, and accent and word substitution on Slavic languages. Unless they are tuned, users report lower quality than Adobe Podcast web and Waves Clarity Vx. — [Adobe community announcement + replies](https://community.adobe.com/announcements-732/now-released-enhance-speech-in-premiere-pro-313485)
- The Mix Amount scale is inconsistent: 0–10 in Essential Sound vs 0–1 in Effect Controls (2024 bug report). — [Adobe community bug](https://community.adobe.com/t5/premiere-pro-beta-bugs/enhance-dialog-effect-inconsistent-nomenclature-and-display-of-dry-wet-mix/idi-p/14421176)

**Auto-ducking (Essential Sound)**
- Enabling ducking adds an **Amplify** effect to the Music or Ambience clip, and "Generate Keyframes" writes the computed gain keyframes into it. Re-generating overwrites manual edits. Parameters: **Duck Against** (Dialogue / Music / SFX / Ambience / Untagged); **Sensitivity** (threshold; "middle-range sensitivity values trigger more adjustments where the music comes in and out quickly between pauses in speech," while high or low values give fewer adjustments); **Duck Amount** (reduction depth); **Fade Duration** (fast for fast music and speech, slow for VO beds); **Fade Position** (outside, inside, or middle of the dialogue). — [Adobe: Automatically duck audio](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/add-audio-effects/adjust-volume-and-levels/automatically-duck-audio.html) (updated Jan 7, 2026)
- Fade Position is new in the 2025+ versions. A third-party guide suggests about −12 dB (subtle) to −18 dB (noticeable) duck amounts, and another tutorial calls the default 800 ms fade "way too slow." Both are low-authority. — [Freepik blog](https://www.freepik.com/blog/how-to-use-auto-ducking-in-premiere-pro-for-clean-professional-audio); [CrumplePop](https://crumplepop.com/audio-ducking-in-premiere-pro-tips-and-advices) (fades in ms, amount in dB; the same fixed reduction applies across the clip)

**Loudness**
- Essential Sound **Auto-Match** sets each clip's overall gain to a fixed loudness, shown under the button in LUFS (the Adobe page shows −23 LUFS). Users confirm the target is not adjustable and that it changes only clip gain, not peaks (use Dynamics/Limiter for that). — [Adobe: Auto-match loudness](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/add-audio-effects/adjust-volume-and-levels/auto-match-audio-loudness.html); [Adobe community](https://community.adobe.com/questions-729/auto-match-loudness-has-no-adjustability-1624392)
- The Loudness Meter has presets based on standards, plus Target Loudness, Maximum True Peak, and a scale of EBU +9 (narrow, broadcast) or EBU +18 (wide, drama/music). Units: LKFS (ITU), LUFS (EBU), LU. — [Adobe: Edit Loudness Meter settings](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/add-audio-effects/apply-audio-effects/edit-loudness-meter-settings.html)
- Export-time **Loudness Normalization** (Effects tab, via Media Encoder) has an adjustable target, tolerance and true-peak limit. Reference standards: EBU R128 −23 LUFS ±0.5 (±1 live), TP −1 dBTP; ATSC A/85 −24 LKFS ±2, TP −2 dBTP. — [ProVideo Coalition LUFS 2018](https://www.provideocoalition.com/lufs-audio-standards-update-for-may-2018/) **[older]**; [Adobe Audition: Match loudness](https://helpx.adobe.com/audition/using/match-loudness.html); [EBU R128 v5](https://tech-qual.ebu.ch/files/live/sites/tech/files/shared/r/r128v5_0.pdf)
- 26.5 fixed a bug where "Deleted ducking keyframes were sometimes still reflected in a sequence's audio output," and an earlier bug where Loudness Normalization processed all streams regardless of the Process checkbox. — [Adobe release notes](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/whats-new/release-notes.html)

### Inferences
- **Reframe:** an equivalent should run a face/saliency detector per frame, then a smoothing stage whose strength is a preset (talking head = near-static crop with very few keyframes, i.e. heavy low-pass or hysteresis on the crop center), and emit keyframes rather than per-frame jitter.
- **Morph Cut:** this is a face-aware frame interpolation over roughly 10–20 frames across a jump cut. Modern frame-interpolation networks (RIFE/FILM-style) with face alignment are the open equivalent, and the same failure modes apply (moving hands or background).
- **Ducking:** sidechain VAD on dialogue → gain envelope on the bed (≈ −12 to −18 dB) with attack/release fades (hundreds of ms) and a choice of fade placement relative to speech. Write it as editable keyframes.
- **Loudness:** Premiere's −23 LUFS auto-match is a broadcast-oriented normalization. Social platforms normalize around −14 LUFS (not verified in this research; see Gaps), so an auto-editor should normalize at export with a true-peak limiter (−1 to −2 dBTP).

### Gaps
- Factory defaults for Duck Amount, Sensitivity and Fade Duration are not published in current Adobe docs. "800 ms" comes from one third-party tutorial; −18 dB and sensitivity 6 are commonly repeated but unverified here.
- The Warp Stabilizer Smoothness default (commonly cited as 50%) is not stated in the page I read.
- The Auto Reframe detector/tracker architecture and the Enhance Speech model architecture are not publicly documented.
- No Adobe source in this research gives platform loudness targets (YouTube, Instagram, TikTok).

## 4. Keyframe interpolation and motion blur / frame blending

### Takeaway
Every AE interpolation is a constrained cubic Bezier. Temporal interpolation (the value/speed graph) and spatial interpolation (the motion path) are independent. The defaults are **Auto Bezier spatial** and Easy Ease at **speed 0 with 33.33% influence** on each side. AE motion blur is sampled multi-subframe blur: shutter angle in degrees (comp default 180°), shutter phase, a minimum of "Samples Per Frame" (used for 3D and shape layers) and an "Adaptive Sample Limit" maximum for 2D layers (long-standing defaults 16 / 128 **[older]**). Frame blending has Frame Mix (cheap cross-fade) and Pixel Motion (optical flow); Premiere's equivalents are Frame Sampling, Frame Blending and Optical Flow.

### Cited Findings
- Temporal interpolation = values in time (value graph: x red, y green, z blue). Spatial interpolation = values in space (motion path). Opacity is temporal-only; Position has both. — [Adobe AE: Keyframe interpolation](https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/desktop/animate-in-after-effects/animation-keyframes/keyframe-interpolation.html) (updated May 5, 2026)
- Methods: Linear (constant rate, "mechanical"); **Bezier** (independent handles; initial handles computed like Auto Bezier); **Auto Bezier** (smooth rate through the key; "the default spatial interpolation"; can produce "boomerang" overshoot between two equal-valued keys, fixed by Hold or Linear); Continuous Bezier (smooth, manually set handles); **Hold** (temporal only, outgoing). Separate incoming and outgoing methods are allowed per key. "All interpolation methods used by After Effects are based on the Bezier interpolation method." A preference can set the default spatial interpolation to Linear. Roving keyframes (Rove Across Time) redistribute keys in time for constant speed. — [Adobe AE: Keyframe interpolation](https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/desktop/animate-in-after-effects/animation-keyframes/keyframe-interpolation.html)
- Speed graph: height = speed. Handle height sets incoming/outgoing speed and handle length sets **influence** (numeric Influence % toward the previous or next key). "After you apply Easy Ease, each keyframe has a speed of 0 with an influence of 33.33% on either side." Shortcuts: F9 Easy Ease, Shift+F9 Ease In, Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+F9 Ease Out. — [Adobe AE: Speed](https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/desktop/animate-in-after-effects/speed-between-keyframes/speed.html); [Adobe AE: Keyboard shortcuts](https://helpx.adobe.com/in/after-effects/desktop/get-started/keyboard-shortcuts/keyboard-shortcuts-reference.html)
- Premiere has the same model (Linear, Bezier, Auto Bezier, Continuous Bezier, Hold, Ease In, Ease Out) in Effect Controls with value/velocity graphs. 25.x "improvements to the Effect Controls panel make keyframing more predictable." — [Adobe Premiere: Keyframe interpolation](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/add-video-effects/control-effects-and-transitions-using-keyframes/control-effect-changes-using-keyframe-interpolation.html); [Adobe release notes](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/whats-new/release-notes.html)
- AE motion blur (Composition Settings → Advanced): Shutter angle in degrees relative to the frame rate (90° at 24 fps = 1/96 s; 1° ≈ none, max 720°). Shutter phase is the offset of shutter open relative to frame start. **Samples Per Frame = the minimum**, used when an adaptive rate can't be determined and for 3D and shape layers. **Adaptive Sample Limit = the maximum**. Sampling adapts per layer to motion. — [Adobe AE: Composition basics](https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/desktop/work-with-compositions/composition-settings/composition-basics.html) (updated Apr 21, 2026)
- **[older]** Defaults: shutter angle 180, phase 0 (CS3+ new comps use phase = −½ angle to center the blur), samples per frame 16, adaptive limit 128. In CS3 the samples-per-frame max was 64 and the adaptive limit max was 256. — [Creative COW forum, 2009](https://creativecow.net/?p=97118); [ProVideo Coalition, Feb 2008](https://www.provideocoalition.com/motion_blur/)
- Frame blending: **Frame Mix** (faster) vs **Pixel Motion** ("much better results, especially for footage that has been drastically slowed down"). Best layer quality gives smoother results, and Draft always uses Frame Mix. Timewarp offers more control. Adobe says to use motion blur, not frame blending, for animated graphics. — [Adobe AE: Time-stretching and time-remapping](https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/desktop/animate-in-after-effects/time-stretching-and-time-remapping/time-stretching-time-remapping.html)
- Premiere time interpolation (speed changes and export frame-rate conversion): **Frame Sampling** (repeat or drop; can stutter), **Frame Blending** (blend neighbors), **Optical Flow** (motion-interpolated new frames; smoothest, but artifacts on large inter-frame differences). — [Adobe Premiere: Time Interpolation](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/edit-projects/change-clip-speed/apply-time-interpolation-methods-to-adjust-clip-speed.html); [Adobe Premiere: Basic video settings](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/render-and-export/export-files/basic-video-settings.html)

### Inferences
- To reproduce AE's Easy Ease exactly in a 1-D property: a cubic Bezier in the (time, value) plane with handle x-length = 0.3333 × segment duration and zero slope at both keys gives `cubic-bezier(0.333, 0, 0.667, 1)` for a symmetric ease between two eased keys. A "pro" look usually comes from stronger influence (60–90%, i.e. an exponential-ish ease), which is what designers set in the graph editor. This is common practice, not an Adobe default.
- Motion blur for generated graphics: render N subframes over shutter = angle/360 × frame duration, centered (phase = −angle/2), with N adapted to on-screen velocity (pixel displacement per frame) up to a cap. That mirrors AE's adaptive sampling and avoids "echo" stepping.

### Gaps
- Current AE versions' default motion-blur values (still 180/−90/16/128?) are not confirmed in the 2026 help page. The defaults above are from 2008–2009 sources.
- Premiere's own Easy Ease influence default is not stated in the docs I read.

## 5. Render quality: Maximum Render Quality, Maximum Bit Depth, 32-bpc linear blending, GPU, scaling, banding

### Takeaway
With GPU acceleration (Metal, CUDA or OpenCL), Premiere always renders effects at 32-bit float in linear light and always uses its high-quality scaler on export. "Use Maximum Render Quality" and "Render at Maximum Depth" mainly matter for CPU-only rendering. The documented scaler chain is Lanczos-2 low-pass + bicubic sampling on GPU, versus variable-radius bicubic on CPU with MRQ (**[older]**, from 2020/CS5-era documentation). AE's quality levers are project bit depth (8/16/32 bpc), a linearized working space, and "Blend colors using 1.0 gamma." Banding is a function of bit depth through the effect chain and of the 8-bit delivery codec.

### Cited Findings
- **Render at Maximum Depth**: renders effects "using the highest bit depth supported by the current format (in most cases, 32-bit float processing)… can help reduce banding artifacts in areas with smooth color gradients." It "may increase encoding times." "Systems with GPU acceleration enabled will always render effects at the highest bit depth available, regardless of this setting." — [Adobe: Basic video settings](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/render-and-export/export-files/basic-video-settings.html) (updated Aug 18, 2026)
- **Use Maximum Render Quality**: "helps preserve details and avoid aliasing when scaling to a frame size different from your source media" (for example 4K → HD) and affects scale, rotation and position transforms. "Systems with supported GPU hardware automatically use maximum render quality if Renderer is set to Metal, CUDA, or OpenCL." — [Adobe: Basic video settings](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/render-and-export/export-files/basic-video-settings.html)
- **[older, 2020 repost of CS5-era article]** Scaling algorithms. CPU: playback bilinear; paused/export Gaussian low-pass + bilinear; MRQ export variable-radius bicubic ("extremely slow"). GPU: playback bilinear; paused/export **Lanczos 2 low-pass sampled with bicubic**, always max quality on export. CPU+MRQ processes in **linear (gamma 1.0) 32 bpc**; "GPU processing is always performed in a 32-bpc linear color space." GPU scaling was >40× faster than CPU in the author's tests. — [Adobe community FAQ (K. Monahan / T. Kopriva)](https://community.adobe.com/t5/premiere-pro/faq-details-about-scaling-quality-and-the-gpu-in-premiere-pro/td-p/11204451)
- Banding reports persist even with both boxes checked. Users fixed specific cases by disabling hardware-accelerated decoding or ProRes hardware encoding, or by exporting at 16 bpc (anecdotal). — [Adobe community: banding in exports](https://community.adobe.com/t5/premiere-pro-discussions/premiere-introduces-banding-in-exports/m-p/8741839); [Adobe community: max depth / MRQ](https://community.adobe.com/t5/premiere-pro-discussions/question-regarding-maximum-render-quality-and-maximum-depth/td-p/10659753)
- Recent Premiere quality fixes: exporting to a smaller frame size could lose quality, and upscaled clips could render at lower quality on reduced-resolution export (26.3). Gaussian Blur "Repeat Edge" could produce lower quality (26.3). 26.5 rebuilt Mosaic softness "for crisper, more natural results." — [Adobe release notes](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/whats-new/release-notes.html)
- Export: Match Source adaptive-bitrate H.264 presets (High, Medium, Low) scale bitrate with frame size. Rewrap presets pass frames through with no re-encode. Time Interpolation applies on frame-rate change. — [Adobe: Basic video settings](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/render-and-export/export-files/basic-video-settings.html)
- AE: "Blend colors using 1.0 gamma" (Project Settings → Color) blends layers in linear light. It affects only inter-layer blending (opacity fades, motion blur, blend modes). A linearized working space "works best with… 16 bpc and 32 bpc, and is not recommended for 8-bpc." It prevents fringing and halos on saturated high-contrast edges. 32 bpc allows over-range values (>1.0, <0). "Convert To Linear Light" defaults to On for 32-bpc output. A 24.6.2 bug made 32-bpc comps export darker. — [Adobe AE: Color management](https://helpx.adobe.com/uk/after-effects/using/color-management.html); [Adobe AE: Fixed issues](https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/desktop/troubleshooting/fixed-and-known-issues/fixed-issues.html)
- AE 25.2 (April 2025): High-Performance Preview Playback streams cached frames disk → RAM. Animated Environment Lights and FBX import for the GPU Advanced 3D renderer. — [Puget Systems, Apr 7 2025](https://www.pugetsystems.com/blog/2025/04/07/adobe-premiere-pro-and-after-effects-whats-new-in-version-25-2/)
- Generative Extend output is 8-bit SDR H.264, so it "may cause color banding in smooth gradients (like sunsets or skin tones)" next to 10-bit sources. — [Adobe: Generative Extend FAQ](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/edit-projects/edit-with-generative-ai/generative-extend-faq.html)

### Inferences
- To match "pro" render quality, an automatic editor should do all compositing, blending, scaling and motion blur in float linear light (convert from Rec.709/sRGB to linear first). It should downscale with a proper low-pass (Lanczos-2/3 or area) rather than bilinear, apply text and graphics at output resolution, and add dither or film-grain noise before quantizing to 8-bit H.264/HEVC to suppress gradient banding (vignettes, skies and soft shadows are the usual banding sources).
- 10-bit HEVC delivery avoids most banding where platforms accept it.

### Gaps
- Adobe has not published a current (2024–2026) description of Premiere's GPU scaling kernel. The Lanczos-2 + bicubic detail is from the CS5-era article reposted in 2020.
- No official statement covers whether current Premiere dithers when quantizing 32-bit float to 8-bit on export.

### Inferences
- The professional baseline in 2026 includes semantic footage search, one-click subject masks with tracking, word-level captions with translation, AI dialogue cleanup, beat-aware music retiming, and short generative fills (≤2 s). An automatic editor competing at this level needs open equivalents: CLIP/SigLIP-style embeddings for search, a SAM-2-style video segmenter for masks, Whisper-class ASR with word timestamps, a speech-enhancement model with a wet/dry mix, and beat-graph music retargeting that preserves intro and outro.
- Adobe's own generative output is 8-bit SDR ≤30 fps, so it is "good enough for social" but not a quality bar. Assistive features define the quality bar more than generative ones.
